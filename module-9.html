2a. What a risk register is

A risk is something that might happen and would hurt the project if it did. A risk register lists each one with the same fields, so leaders can compare them:

FieldWhat it means
Likelihood (1 to 5)How likely it is to happen before or soon after go-live. 1 = rare, 5 = almost certain.
Impact (1 to 5)How bad it would be for patients, money, or the schedule. 1 = minor, 5 = severe.
ScoreLikelihood × impact, from 1 to 25. Higher scores get attention first.
OwnerOne named person who watches the risk and acts on it. Not a committee.
ResponseAvoid (change the plan so it can't happen), reduce (make it less likely or less harmful), transfer (shift the cost, for example to insurance or a contract), or accept (live with it, on purpose, with a backup plan).
Warning sign and triggerA measure you can watch, and the level at which the owner must act.

Scoring likelihood and impact is a standard way to assess risk (NIST Special Publication 800-30, Guide for Conducting Risk Assessments, 2012). The score is a judgment, so write down the evidence behind it.

Practice question (not graded)

A risk has likelihood 2 and impact 5. Another has likelihood 4 and impact 3. Which scores higher, and what does that tell you?

2b. Security risk rises during a transition

A move to a new EHR creates extra accounts, vendor connections, copies of data, and unusual emails, which is what attackers look for. Healthcare breaches cost an average of $7.42 million in 2025 and took about 279 days to identify and contain, the longest of any industry (IBM, Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025, as reported July 30, 2025). When the claims company Change Healthcare was attacked in 2024, 94% of nearly 1,000 hospitals in an American Hospital Association survey reported a financial impact (AHA, March 15, 2024). The Joint Commission tells hospitals to be ready to run with key technology offline for four weeks or longer, with downtime plans, trained teams, and practice drills (Sentinel Event Alert 67, August 2023).

Practice question (not graded)

Which of Kevin's risks does a downtime drill reduce?

2c. The cash dip after go-live

Hospitals often feel a money squeeze right after go-live. One large cancer center reported a temporary drop in patient activity in the two months after its February 2025 go-live and an operating loss for the half year, partly from go-live costs (Becker's Hospital Review, Aug. 19, 2025). A 25-bed Iowa hospital reported a one-month loss of $430,135 after its 2025 go-live, including about $500,000 of implementation costs (Becker's, Aug. 14, 2025). Slower claims are usually delayed money, not lost money, but the bills still come due. That is why days of cash on hand matters: the median for not-for-profit acute care providers was about 208 days in S&P's preliminary 2025 figures, with a median operating margin of about 1.2% (Becker's, June 15, 2026).

Practice question (not graded)

KFCH spends about $375 million a year, or about $1.03 million a day. If $29 million arrives late, about how many days of cash does that use up?

2d. Hidden dependencies

Software runs on power, cooling, network closets, and people's hands. A cut to Facilities, a crew too small to install 180 workstations, or a team that was never asked can stop a go-live as surely as a software bug. Good registers look past the project's own budget for these dependencies.

2e. Reading the warning signs

A warning sign (a leading indicator) moves before the harm happens. A lagging measure tells you after. Rising vendor accounts and a growing coding backlog are warning signs; a breach report or a missed payroll is lagging. For each risk, pick a measure that moves early, and a trigger level that makes the owner act.

Practice question (not graded)

In the measures file, the coding backlog went from 4.0 to 6.0 days between October and March. What is the best use of that number?

Step 4 · Copilot practice · 60 min

This week's technique: give permission to say "I don't know"

AI tools try to answer every question, so when the facts run out they may guess and sound sure. You can reduce that by telling Copilot, in your prompt, that it is fine to say it doesn't know: for example, to mark any score or fact the files don't support as "not in the source" or "uncertain," instead of filling the gap. Then you can see where your own judgment is needed.

Sources: Anthropic, "Prompt engineering best practices for 2026" (published November 10, 2025); Microsoft Learn, "Write prompts that get results" (updated October 2026). You write your own prompts in this course; none are provided to copy.

Worked example, on a different made-up case

Imagine a clinic manager who asks an AI tool to list the risks of moving her lab to a new building and score each one. Here is how it could go. The first answer might be a neat table with a confident "likelihood 4" for flooding, even though nothing in her notes mentions water. So she asks again and adds: "Only use my notes. If my notes don't support a score, write 'uncertain' and say what fact you would need." Now scores her notes don't support should come back "uncertain," such as flooding ("need: the building's flood history"). Those are the ones she looks up herself.

  3. Check every row against the files: Is each fact really in the memos or the measures? Did Copilot invent a number? Which scores did it mark uncertain, and which should it have?

Step 7 · Ethics exercise · 40 min

ACHE Code of Ethics, Section VI: The Healthcare Executive's Responsibility to Report Violations of the Code

Read Section VI of the ACHE Code of Ethics (as amended Dec. 8, 2025). It says that an ACHE member who has reasonable grounds to believe another member has violated the Code must report the facts to ACHE's Ethics Committee in a timely way. Section I (responsibilities to the profession), which asks leaders to be honest and to avoid misleading others, also applies.
