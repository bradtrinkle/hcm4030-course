Step 2 · Polish the deck · 100 min

Start from your Module 12 deck. Use your coach feedback and any instructor feedback you have. Then go through this list.

  • Recommendation first. The slide after the title says what the board is being asked to approve and what it costs.
  • Message titles. Every slide title is a sentence that says the point, not a topic.
  • Numbers match. Every cost and date matches the board briefing and your earlier modules. Recheck each total by hand.
  • The chair's five questions are each answered somewhere in the deck.
  • Readable. No slide needs more than about a minute to explain. Text is large enough to read on a phone screen.
  • Honest. The hard parts (the productivity dip, the cash dip, staff strain) are on the slides with what you'll do about them.

You may use Copilot to check the deck, for example to list every number on your slides so you can trace each one to its source. You write your own prompts, and you check what it gives back.

Step 3 · Prepare for the board's questions · 60 min

The trustees sent these questions ahead. On your video, you'll answer Question A and one other of your choice. Read each question aloud, then answer it in about 2 minutes, the way you would in the room: a direct answer first, then the reason, then what you'll do.

  • Question A · The chair (required): "If the first two weeks after go-live go badly, what exactly will you do, and who makes the call?"
  • Question B · The retired nurse: "What will be different for a night-shift nurse on the first night, and who does she call at 3 a.m.?"
  • Question C · The family physician from Prairie Bend: "My patients see me at the clinic and end up admitted here. How does their record follow them through go-live?"
  • Question D · The farm co-op manager: "Add up everything you're asking for on top of the $47 million. What's the total, and what would you cut first if the money isn't there?"
  • Question E · A county resident who is a patient here: "How will patients like me find out what's changing, and where do we go if something is wrong with our record or our bill?"
  • Question F · The chief of the medical staff: "How will you keep doctors from drowning in alerts and clicks after go-live, and how will you know if it's happening?"

Good answers are honest about what you don't know yet and say how you'll find out. You may use Copilot to play a skeptical trustee and question you, with your own prompt. Don't read a Copilot answer on camera; the board wants your judgment.

Step 4 · Record · 140 min

How to record

  1. Rehearse once out loud with a timer before you record. Aim for 8 to 11 minutes for the deck.
  2. Record in PowerPoint (the desktop app in your Microsoft 365 plan): open your deck and select Record. Your voice and your slides are recorded together. Turn your camera on if you can; if not, your voice over the slides is fine.
  3. Record the questions too. After your last slide, add one slide that shows each question (or show it on an appendix slide), read it aloud, and answer it.
  4. Export as a video: File > Export > Create a Video. Choose HD (720p) to keep the file smaller, keep Use Recorded Timings and Narrations, then save as MPEG-4 (.mp4).
  5. Watch it once all the way through before you submit. Check the sound and that every slide shows.

Another recorder is fine (for example, a Teams meeting recorded with only you in it) as long as the board can see your slides and hear you, and you submit an .mp4. If Canvas won't take your video because of its size, upload it to your ISU OneDrive, share a link your instructor can open, and paste the link below.

PowerPoint's steps come from Microsoft Support, "Record your presentation" and "Turn your presentation into a video" (2025 to 2026). Menus can move; if yours look different, search the PowerPoint help for "Record".
