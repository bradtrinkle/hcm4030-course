Step 2 · Polish the deck · 100 min

Start from your Module 12 deck. Use your coach feedback and any instructor feedback you have. Then go through this list.

  • Recommendation first. The slide after the title says what the board is being asked to approve and what it costs.
  • Message titles. Every slide title is a sentence that says the point, not a topic.
  • Numbers match. Every cost and date matches the board briefing and your earlier modules. Recheck each total by hand.
  • The chair's five questions are each answered somewhere in the deck.
  • Readable. No slide needs more than about a minute to explain. Text is large enough to read on a phone screen.
  • Honest. The hard parts (the productivity dip, the cash dip, staff strain) are on the slides with what you'll do about them.

You may use Copilot to check the deck, for example to list every number on your slides so you can trace each one to its source. You write your own prompts, and you check what it gives back.

Step 3 · Prepare for the board's questions · 60 min

The trustees sent these questions ahead. On your video, you'll answer Question A and one other of your choice. Read each question aloud, then answer it in about 2 minutes, the way you would in the room: a direct answer first, then the reason, then what you'll do.

  • Question A · The chair (required): "If the first two weeks after go-live go badly, what exactly will you do, and who makes the call?"
  • Question B · The retired nurse: "What will be different for a night-shift nurse on the first night, and who does she call at 3 a.m.?"
  • Question C · The family physician from Prairie Bend: "My patients see me at the clinic and end up admitted here. How does their record follow them through go-live?"
  • Question D · The farm co-op manager: "Add up everything you're asking for on top of the $47 million. What's the total, and what would you cut first if the money isn't there?"
  • Question E · A county resident who is a patient here: "How will patients like me find out what's changing, and where do we go if something is wrong with our record or our bill?"
  • Question F · The chief of the medical staff: "How will you keep doctors from drowning in alerts and clicks after go-live, and how will you know if it's happening?"

Good answers are honest about what you don't know yet and say how you'll find out. You may use Copilot to play a skeptical trustee and question you, with your own prompt. Don't read a Copilot answer on camera; the board wants your judgment.

Step 4 · Record in Canvas Studio · 140 min

You'll record your screen and your webcam at the same time with Canvas Studio, which is built into Canvas and free for ISU students. The board sees your slides full size and you in a small box in the corner. Plan on one practice run, one or two real takes, and time to watch the result.

4a. Get ready (about 30 minutes)

  1. Use a laptop or desktop with Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge. Studio's own recorder works in those two browsers. Don't use a phone. (In Safari or Firefox, Studio asks you to install a separate app, Screencast-o-Matic; it's simpler to use Chrome or Edge.)
  2. Find a quiet room. A headset or earbuds with a microphone sounds better than a laptop's built-in mic. Turn off notifications and close email and chat so nothing pops up on screen.
  3. Add a questions slide at the end of your deck (after the last main slide, before the appendix) that shows Question A and your second question. It doesn't count toward the 10-slide limit.
  4. Rehearse once out loud with a timer. Aim for 8 to 11 minutes for the deck.
  5. Have both windows open: your deck in PowerPoint, and Canvas in Chrome or Edge.

4b. Record (about 80 minutes, including a practice take and retakes)

  1. In Canvas, click Studio in the red menu on the far left of the screen (the main Canvas menu, under Inbox and History). It opens My Library.
  2. Click Create (top right), then Studio Capture.
  3. If you see a Screen Capture drop-down, change it to Enabled. If your browser asks to use your camera and microphone, click Allow.
  4. Click the Entire Screen tab, click the picture of your screen, and click Share. Choose Entire Screen, not Window or Chrome Tab: it's the only choice that adds your webcam. If you have two monitors, pick the one where your slide show will play.
  5. When the Enable Camera window appears, click Yes. Your webcam appears in a small box. Drag it to a corner where it won't cover slide text; drag its corner to resize it.
  6. Click Start Recording.
  7. Switch to PowerPoint and start the slide show: Slide Show > From Beginning. Pause a second, then start talking.
  8. Present your deck. On the questions slide, read Question A aloud and answer it, then read your second question and answer it.
  9. Press Esc to leave the slide show, switch back to the Studio tab, and click Finish Recording. (Clicking Stop sharing also ends the recording.) If you need a break mid-way, Pause and then Continue work too.
  10. Click Play to preview. If it went badly, Start Over deletes it so you can record again.
  11. In the Title box, type Final board presentation - Your Name, then click Save Media. The video appears in My Library. Wait until it finishes processing (it can take several minutes) before you submit.

4c. Check it (about 30 minutes)

Open the video from My Library and watch it all the way through. Check that your voice is clear, every slide shows, your webcam box doesn't hide anything important, and the length is within the limit. If not, record it again; only the video you submit counts.

If Studio won't work for you: record in PowerPoint instead (select Record, record the deck and your answers, then File > Export > Create a Video, choose HD (720p), and save as MPEG-4). Then submit with the File Upload option in Step 5. For Studio problems, use Canvas Help (the ? in the main Canvas menu) or the ISU Solution Center at 515-294-4000, and start early so there's time to get help before May 13.

Sources for these steps: Iowa State CELT, "Canvas Studio"; Instructure guides "How do I record a screen capture video in Studio with a Chrome or Edge browser?" and "How do I submit Studio media as a Text Entry assignment in Canvas as a student?"; Microsoft Support, "Turn your presentation into a video". Screens can change; if a button looks different, look for the closest match.

How to submit in Canvas (one submission, three parts)

  1. Open the Final: Board presentation assignment in Canvas and click Start Assignment.
  2. Choose the Text Entry option.
  3. In the editor's toolbar, click the blue Studio icon. Find your video in My Library, click its picture, and click Embed Media. The video appears in the box.
  4. Click below the video. In the toolbar, click the Documents icon (a page with lines), then Upload Document, and upload your deck (.pptx). Do the same for the Word file you just downloaded.
  5. Check that the video and both file links show in the box, then click Submit Assignment.

When you submit, your instructor gets a copy of your video, and you can't change its title afterward. PowerPoint backup: choose File Upload instead and upload all three files (deck, .mp4, and Word file) in one submission. Due Thursday, May 13, 11:59 pm Central. One attempt; no late work.
