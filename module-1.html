3a. What a healthcare information system is

A healthcare information system is more than software. It is people, processes, technology, and data working together so the right information reaches the right person at the right time. When a system fails, the cause is often a process or a person, not the code.

3b. Clinical systems

The electronic health record (EHR) is the center: notes, orders, results, medications, and problem lists. Two parts matter most to leaders: order entry (clinicians enter orders for tests and medicines directly) and clinical decision support (alerts and reminders, such as a drug-allergy warning). Around the EHR sit specialty systems: the imaging archive (PACS), the laboratory information system, and the pharmacy system. Each sends information to and from the EHR through interfaces.

Practice question (not graded)

A nurse orders a medicine in the EHR, and a warning pops up about the patient's drug allergy. Which part of the EHR is doing that?

3c. Administrative and financial systems

Revenue cycle systems turn care into payment: registration, insurance checks, coding, claims, and collections. ERP (enterprise resource planning) systems run finance, HR, payroll, scheduling, and supplies. These systems rarely make headlines, but if they stop, a hospital can run out of cash or supplies.

3d. E-Health, the Internet, and connecting systems

E-Health means care and health information delivered over the Internet: patient portals, telehealth video visits, and remote monitoring. Interoperability is the ability of different systems, often from different vendors, to exchange and use information. It depends on shared standards; a widely used modern one is HL7 FHIR. A health information exchange lets organizations in a region share patient records.

Practice question (not graded)

Which of these is an E-Health tool?

3e. Hardware, networks, and security

Every system runs on hardware (servers, workstations, tablets, medical devices) and networks (wired, Wi-Fi, and Internet links), either in the hospital's own data center or in the cloud. In the United States, HIPAA requires hospitals to protect the privacy and security of patients' health information. Downtime plans matter: when systems go down, staff fall back on paper.

3f. AI in healthcare information systems

AI already appears inside many systems: tools that draft a visit note from the conversation (often called ambient documentation or an ambient scribe), models that flag patients at risk, and chat assistants. AI can save time, but it can be wrong. A person stays responsible: the clinician who signs the note owns what it says.

Practice question (not graded)

An AI tool drafts a doctor's visit note from the conversation in the exam room. Who is responsible for the note being accurate?

3g. Planning, project management, and the future

Big system changes are projects: they need a sponsor, a team, a budget, a timeline, and a plan for people, not just technology. That is what the rest of this course is about. Looking ahead, expect more AI, more care at home, and more data shared across organizations.

Step 4 · Copilot practice · 45 min

This week's technique: zero-shot prompting

A zero-shot prompt is a plain request with no examples and no extra structure: you simply ask. It is how most people start with AI, and it often works for simple tasks. Its weakness is that the AI fills every gap you leave with its own guesses: who the answer is for, how long it should be, and which facts to use. This week you practice asking plainly and then spotting the guesses. In Module 2 you will learn a four-part prompt that closes those gaps.

Source: IBM, "The 2026 Guide to Prompt Engineering" (published July 23, 2025), which names zero-shot prompting. You write your own prompts in this course; none are provided to copy.

Worked example, on a different made-up case

A new clinic manager asked Copilot, with no other detail, to explain what a nurse call system does. The answer was accurate but written for engineers, ran four paragraphs, and described features her clinic does not have. Nothing was "wrong", yet it did not fit her need. She listed what Copilot had guessed (the reader, the length, the features) and added those details to her second request.

Step 7 · Ethics exercise · 40 min

ACHE Code of Ethics, Section V: The Healthcare Executive's Responsibilities to Community and Society

Read Section V of the ACHE Code of Ethics (as amended Dec. 8, 2025). Two items matter most here: item 5, about giving patients adequate, accurate information to make informed decisions, and item 6, about supporting access to healthcare for all people, especially the underserved.

The case
To cut costs, a manager proposes that Kestrel Falls stop mailing paper test results and move most clinic follow-up visits to telehealth. Results would be available only in the patient portal. Walter Engstrom, 81, lives on a farm outside town with unreliable Internet and does not use the portal. His daughter Beth helps him when she can, but she lives two hours away. Many of the clinics' older and rural patients are in a similar spot.
